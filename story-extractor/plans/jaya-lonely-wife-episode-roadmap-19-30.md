## B. Escalation levels (non-graphic scene-planning shorthand)

- **B0 — Contact only:** meeting, talk, visible distance; no deliberately intimate touch.
- **B1 — Social proximity:** sitting or walking close, brief ordinary contact, one person deliberately pulling away.
- **B2 — Chosen gentle contact:** mutually accepted handhold or touch on arm, followed by an emotional reaction.
- **B3 — Emotional embrace:** voluntarily initiated hug/held closeness; tension belongs to the aftermath, not anatomical description.
- **B4 — Kiss:** mutually desired and explicitly chosen adult romantic contact; scene can cut before further action.
- **B5 — Private intimacy implied:** two established adults deliberately choose privacy; **fade to black**. No mechanical or anatomical prose in this plan.

An episode's label means its **maximum achieved boundary**, not an obligation to escalate at every appearance. **Reversal, distance and restraint count as changes in the physical relationship.** The Stranger is an antagonist/risk thread, never a consensual-romance escalation meter.

## C. Proposed next 12 episodes — three-episode pacing clusters

### Cluster 1: Aftermath and re-entry (EP19–21)

#### EP19 — The Morning That Doesn't Feel Ordinary
- **Enters:** Jaya, Mani (existing), Anil; Rakesh appears through a scheduled voice call.
- **Why this episode:** Begin immediately after the already-established Jaya–Mani focus, not with a sudden unrelated male lead. Follow Jaya through a normal morning and teaching preparation while she processes what has changed and what others might have noticed.
- **Major sequence:** domestic routine → unfinished contact with Mani → she postpones a conversation rather than rewriting the past → Anil's ordinary household request interrupts → Rakesh calls from Qatar.
- **Relationship/physical ceiling:** Jaya–Mani **B0** for new material; Jaya–Rakesh remains long-distance, verbal familiarity only. No invented physical event while Mani's status and last scene are unverified.
- **Exit hook:** a college notice arrives just as Jaya thinks she has recovered control of the day.

#### EP20 — Someone Notices the Difference
- **Enters:** Megha (new), Jaya; Stranger via a brief unexpected notification.
- **Major sequence:** timetable change makes Megha and Jaya share a duty → Jaya misses a small detail → Megha helps without demanding personal history → after work an ambiguous familiar message disturbs Jaya.
- **Reason:** Reintroduce the work world, establish *friendship before confidential disclosure*, bring back an old narrative device (private messaging).
- **Physical ceiling:** Jaya–Megha **B0–B1** ordinary friendly proximity; no romantic beat. Stranger: no physical appearance.
- **Exit hook:** Megha quietly asks whether Jaya wants company on the way home, and Jaya does not answer immediately.

#### EP21 — The Visiting Coordinator
- **Enters:** Dev (new), Megha, Jaya, a school administrator.
- **Major sequence:** workshop announcement → Dev's practical timetable clashes with Jaya's teaching slot → Jaya challenges an assumption → Dev takes her concern seriously → an unexpected moment of professional respect.
- **Reason:** Introduce Dev as *a person with work*, not as immediate romance or a mysterious rescue.
- **Physical ceiling:** Jaya–Dev **B0**. Their only escalation is mutual attention and remembered details.
- **Exit hook:** Jaya is assigned to coordinate the next session alongside Dev.

### Cluster 2: Routine proximity and competing loyalties (EP22–24)

#### EP22 — The Second Meeting
- **Enters:** Jaya, Dev, Megha; Anil at home.
- **Major sequence:** workshop setup → delayed projector/room change → Dev and Jaya work together without audience → ordinary accidental closeness → Jaya steps away after a call from home.
- **Why now:** Repeat contact in different but plausible situations before attachment.
- **Physical ceiling:** Jaya–Dev **B1**, ordinary accidental contact; **no kiss or embrace**.
- **Exit hook:** Dev notices her hesitation but does not interrogate her.

#### EP23 — A Different Kind of Trust
- **Enters:** Jaya, Megha, Dev; Mrs. D'Souza introduced briefly near apartment entrance.
- **Major sequence:** Megha mentions local book/event meet → Dev arrives as a workshop contact, not a setup date → conversation reveals something about his life and Jaya's teaching ambitions → Jaya is reminded of the apartment's visitor/security routine on return.
- **Reason:** Increase conversational confidence while simultaneously bringing existing privacy anxiety into view.
- **Physical ceiling:** Jaya–Dev **B1**; a hand offered to help with books is accepted only momentarily, not converted into a seduction sequence.
- **Exit hook:** Mrs. D'Souza asks Jaya whether a service visitor in the building belonged to her flat.

#### EP24 — A Call That Changes the Calendar
- **Enters:** Rakesh on call, Jaya, Mani as a pending unanswered message, Dev in a brief professional context.
- **Major sequence:** Rakesh discusses leave/possible travel, without confirming arrival → Jaya tries to hold normal conversation → she declines a casual invitation to stay after a workshop → late-night unresolved messages reappear.
- **Why:** A consequential **de-escalation**. Work-world attraction becomes harder precisely because marriage remains real.
- **Physical ceiling:** Jaya–Dev **B0** (intentional distance after B1). Jaya–Rakesh: voice-only tenderness; Mani: no new physical beat.
- **Exit hook:** Rakesh says he will confirm his leave date, leaving Jaya with a decision rather than a revelation.

### Cluster 3: Choice, close contact, and exposure (EP25–27)

#### EP25 — What an Ordinary Favor Means
- **Enters:** Jaya, Dev, Megha, Anil.
- **Major sequence:** a manageable school emergency causes Jaya to stay late → Dev helps complete the immediate work, with Megha present for context → Jaya thanks him privately → a deliberate but brief handhold occurs and she is the first to release it.
- **Why:** A step that cannot be dismissed as an accident; she now has to interpret her own choice.
- **Physical ceiling:** Jaya–Dev **B2**. No further escalation this day.
- **Exit hook:** Jaya deliberately deletes an unsent text, not evidence of anyone else's wrongdoing.

#### EP26 — The Line Named Aloud
- **Enters:** Jaya, Dev; Megha nearby but not reading their minds; Stranger remains only a remote pressure thread.
- **Major sequence:** small follow-up school task → Dev notes growing attachment without demanding an answer → Jaya identifies her marriage and uncertainty → one moment of mutual closeness becomes a voluntarily initiated **embrace**, then they part.
- **Why:** Emotional consent and restraint must be legible. Their choice creates future consequences.
- **Physical ceiling:** Jaya–Dev **B3**. **No further private intimacy**.
- **Exit hook:** Rakesh sends a confirmed travel window, and she sees it after returning home.

#### EP27 — Information Is Unequal
- **Enters:** Mrs. D'Souza, Jaya, Megha, Rakesh by video call; Dev absent in person.
- **Major sequence:** apartment committee flags an incomplete visitor-log entry → Jaya asks what is actually known → Megha supports her without accepting wild conclusions → Rakesh calls to coordinate travel → Jaya pauses before replying to Dev.
- **Reason:** Swing the story away from uninterrupted physical escalation. Existing Stranger/watchman/privacy threads deserve consequences.
- **Physical ceiling:** Jaya–Dev **B0** (no meeting); marriage remains remote communication. Suspicion itself is the escalation.
- **Exit hook:** someone contacts Jaya from an unfamiliar number, but the sender is **not identified**.

### Cluster 4: Overlap and the next open branch (EP28–30)

#### EP28 — One More Conversation
- **Enters:** Jaya, Dev, Megha, school administrator; Rakesh offscreen preparing to travel.
- **Major sequence:** workshop winds down → Dev and Jaya talk honestly about what the embrace meant → she chooses to end their meeting before a private kiss → Megha sees them leaving separately, not a scandal.
- **Reason:** Adult attraction may progress without being automatic; preserve Jaya's agency and narrative suspense.
- **Physical ceiling:** Jaya–Dev **B2** chosen hand contact at most, **down from earlier B3**.
- **Exit hook:** Dev's project extension means he need not disappear; Jaya now has to decide how much distance to establish.

#### EP29 — The Return
- **Enters:** Rakesh in Delhi after an established travel interval, Jaya, Anil; optional family phone call with Reema.
- **Major sequence:** routine airport/travel coordination → reunion in a familiar domestic location → initial formality yields to an affectionate embrace → Jaya and Rakesh acknowledge how long they have been apart without instantly resolving everything.
- **Reason:** Bring the marriage back onstage as a living relationship, not merely a threat to a new pairing.
- **Physical ceiling:** Jaya–Rakesh **B3** (mutual embrace); Jaya–Dev no physical meeting.
- **Exit hook:** a small discrepancy in Jaya's account of recent weeks prompts an unanswered question from Rakesh.

#### EP30 — Two Unfinished Truths
- **Enters:** Jaya, Rakesh, Megha; Dev appears only through a missed message, Stranger through a separate old thread.
- **Major sequence:** married domestic routine resumes, but with new awkwardness → Jaya and Rakesh regain some affectionate ease → **one mutually chosen kiss**; any greater intimacy remains a *possible later scene*, not an automatic beat → at school Megha raises an issue of honesty and reputation → Jaya receives an unrelated notification that reactivates the old Stranger concern.
- **Why:** End *this planning tranche* at a higher level of emotional conflict, not with a solution to the marriage, Stranger, Mani, or Dev arcs.
- **Physical ceiling:** Jaya–Rakesh **B4** (non-graphic consensual kiss). Jaya–Dev no new contact. **B5 is deliberately not reached here** because the mutual context would be rushed.
- **Exit hook:** Jaya has to decide *which person to speak to first*, while the unknown sender's identity and actual evidence remain unknown.

**No final chapter. No solved Stranger reveal. No forced moral ending. EP31 onward is intentionally open.**

## E. Style transfer — architecture, not imitation of exact sentences

The source's recurring construction is grounded in *daily time, college/house transitions, calls/messages, doors/arrival interruptions, dialogue and a chapter-end disturbance*. Mirror the **architecture** rather than copying original phrases:

1. **Start in the residue of the previous episode.** Carry forward physical location, emotional aftereffect and a specific unfinished question; do not time-jump merely for more action.
2. **Establish ordinary schedule first:** morning commute, faculty duties, home chores or a family call.
3. **Keep speech and internal reaction in short alternation.** Show hesitation through silence, pauses and choices.
4. **Give a plausible reason for each arrival.** Nobody is at Jaya's home or workplace just because the outline needs a scene.
5. **Make relationship stages observable:** distance → awareness → mutual choice → immediate aftereffect; never treat every meeting as a guaranteed increase.
6. **Use familiar interrupting mechanisms:** caller ID, a doorbell, visitor book, a school notice, a family responsibility. Maintain separate knowledge states for each character.
7. **Do not overwrite source canon with predictions.** The unknown Stranger's identity, Mani's precise final state/adult status, and any other unresolved detail remain OPEN until verified in the actual source.
